# ADR-0005: The incident register publishes verbatim

**Status:** Accepted (2026-07-13; reconstructed 2026-08-13)

## Context

`fleet-reports/incidents.md` is a public register of post-mortems, with full write-ups
under `fleet-reports/incidents/`. Those write-ups carry **homelab-internal architecture
detail** — container IDs, Proxmox / `pct` / `root@pam` / `vzdump` mechanics, hostnames.
This repo is **public**. The obvious instinct is to sanitize before publishing, the way
the weekly `fleet-report.md` is scoped to public metadata only ("no homelab-internal
detail").

The question is whether the incident reports should follow the same sanitize-first rule
or keep their detail.

## Decision

**Publish the incident reports verbatim.** Owner call (Chris, 2026-07-13): the
homelab-internal specificity *is* the CTO-methodology value the register exists to
demonstrate, and stripping it would gut the reports. The register and the weekly report
partition by purpose — `fleet-report.md` is the sanitized public-metadata sibling;
`incidents.md` is the detailed one.

The one **hard line**: never a credential, key, private IP-plus-purpose map, or secret.
The review before a report lands is that eyeball — checking for those — **not**
sanitizing the architecture. Reports are harvested from session transcripts by the local
`/incident-digest` playbook and land via local PRs; the register itself is generated by
`metrics/generate-fleet-reports.py`, never hand-edited (`ci/validate.py`'s `register`
check asserts reproducibility).

**Companion integrity decisions** — the same "publish fleet records honestly and safely"
posture, recorded alongside:

- **The census counts only git-tracked files (`cloc --vcs=git`).** `cloc` does not honor
  `.gitignore`; a raw working-tree scan counts whatever sits on the maintainer's disk. At
  the 2026-07-25 census refresh an untracked personal Spotify export measured ~1.6
  million JSON lines — nineteen times the entire previous census — and would have been
  published as fleet source. Counting only what git tracks is the durable fix.
- **`cloc` is pinned by sha256 (#78 / #80).** The runner's apt `cloc` (1.98) silently
  drops `.astro` and Jinja templates from the census; the workflow fetches the tagged
  `cloc-2.06.pl` and verifies it against a recorded checksum so CI and local regens stay
  reproducible.
- **"Prove a check can fail before trusting it."** A check that cannot fail reads as
  coverage while asserting nothing; adding one means deliberately breaking the thing,
  confirming a red run, and restoring.

## Alternatives

- **Sanitize the architecture detail, like `fleet-report.md` (recorded — the rejected
  default).** Rejected. The detail is the value, and the sanitized public sibling already
  exists for the audience that wants metadata only. Applying the same rule to both would
  make the register redundant.
- **Keep incidents in a private repo or an internal-only register (retrospective — not
  considered at the time).** *Worse.* It defeats the entire purpose: the register exists
  to demonstrate the operating methodology *in the open*. Privacy is already achieved for
  the genuinely sensitive material by the credential/key/IP-map hard line, without hiding
  the whole thing.
- **Redact hostnames but keep everything else (retrospective — not considered at the
  time).** *Lateral, and not worth the friction.* Hostnames alone are not the exposure —
  a private IP mapped to its purpose is, and that is already the hard line. Blanket
  hostname redaction adds per-report editing toil and dents the specificity that makes
  the reports useful, in exchange for guarding something the existing rule does not treat
  as the real risk.

## Consequences

- Incident reports ship with their homelab-internal architecture intact; the pre-merge
  review is the credential/key/private-IP-purpose-map eyeball, applied per report.
- `incidents.md` is generated from `fleet-reports/incidents/` and CI-checked for
  reproducibility; a hand-edit fails the `register` check.
- The census (`metrics/language-census.md` and the report's re-cut) reflects only
  git-tracked source, pinned to a known `cloc`, so published line counts are honest and
  reproducible rather than an artifact of one disk.
